# User Personas

## Overview
These personas represent our target users for NewMoji. They're based on Gen Z and Gen Alpha news consumption patterns, information literacy challenges, and digital behavior. Each persona helps us design for real user needs and validate our product decisions.

---

## Persona 1: "Social Media Native Zoe"

### Demographics
- **Age:** 20
- **Occupation:** College sophomore (Communications major)
- **Location:** Urban area, lives on campus
- **Tech:** iPhone 14, uses phone 6+ hours/day
- **Social media:** Instagram (daily), TikTok (multiple times daily), Twitter/X (occasionally), Snapchat (for friends)

### News Consumption Behavior

**Current habits:**
- Gets news through TikTok's For You Page (70% of news exposure)
- Follows creators who comment on news (not news organizations)
- Reads headlines on Instagram stories from friends' reposts
- Clicks through to articles maybe 10% of the time
- Reads Reddit threads for explanations of complex topics
- Never visits news websites directly

**Information diet:**
- 📱 Screen time: 6-7 hours/day
- 📰 Time on "news": 30-45 min/day (but doesn't realize it)
- 🎥 Content preference: Video > Image > Text
- ⚡ Attention span: 15 seconds to hook, 2-3 min max for deep dives

**Pain points:**
- "I don't trust mainstream media but idk who to trust"
- "Everything is so depressing, I just want to tune it out"
- "I feel like I should know what's happening but it's overwhelming"
- "All my friends have different versions of what happened"
- "I can't tell what's real vs what's exaggerated for clicks"

### Motivations & Goals

**Why she'd use NewMoji:**
- ✅ Wants to be informed without feeling overwhelmed
- ✅ Cares about social issues and wants to discuss them intelligently
- ✅ Feels pressure to "stay current" with friends and classmates
- ✅ Interested in forming her own opinions (not just following creators)
- ✅ Wants to avoid looking uninformed or gullible

**What would make her return:**
- 🎯 Bite-sized, easy to digest
- 🎨 Visually appealing, feels native to her digital experience
- 🏆 Gamification that doesn't feel childish
- 🤝 Shareable content that makes her look informed
- 📈 Visible progress (seeing her media literacy improve)
- ⚡ Takes < 10 minutes per session

### Digital Behavior Patterns

**App usage:**
- Checks phone immediately upon waking
- Scrolls TikTok during meals, between classes, before bed
- Responds to notifications instantly
- Deletes apps she doesn't use weekly
- Loves apps with streaks (Duolingo, Snapchat)

**Content engagement:**
- Scrolls fast, stops for anything eye-catching
- Watches videos without sound first (always has captions on)
- Double-taps to like quickly, rarely comments
- Shares content via DMs more than public posting
- Judges credibility by creator's vibe, not source

**Learning style:**
- Visual > textual
- Learns by doing, not reading instructions
- Responds well to challenges and achievements
- Likes feeling smart when she "gets" something

### Quotes
- "I literally get all my news from TikTok and honestly it's fine?"
- "I know I should read actual articles but they're so boring and long"
- "Sometimes I'll share something and then find out it was fake and I feel so dumb"
- "My professor asked about a news story and I had no idea what he was talking about"
- "I care about what's happening in the world, I just don't know where to start"

### NewMoji Experience (How she'd use it)

**Discovery:**
- Friend shares an emoji headline on their Instagram story
- Clicks through, sees the app interface
- "Wait this is actually cool"

**First session:**
- Scrolls through emoji headlines
- Taps one that catches her eye: `🏛️⚖️🚫 Abortion ruling`
- Reads Level 1 (headline), taps for more
- Gets intrigued by the "spot the bias" challenge
- Earns first badge
- "Okay this is actually interesting"

**Return behavior:**
- Gets a notification: "Your 2-day streak! 🔥"
- Opens app while waiting for class
- Completes daily challenge (read opposing perspective)
- Shares her media literacy score to her story
- Friends ask what app it is

**Long-term:**
- Checks NewMoji every morning (part of routine)
- Has a 47-day streak
- Actually starts fact-checking things she sees on TikTok
- Recommends it to friends when they share questionable news

### Design Implications
- Mobile-first, vertical scrolling
- Emoji headlines must be actually eye-catching
- Progressive disclosure should feel like TikTok (tap to see more)
- Gamification needs to be shareable to social media
- Quick win in first 60 seconds or she bounces
- Dark mode by default
- Minimal text, maximum visual hierarchy

## Persona 2: "Checked Out Cameron"

### Demographics
- **Age:** 17
- **Occupation:** High school senior
- **Location:** Suburban area, lives with parents
- **Tech:** Android phone (Samsung), gaming PC
- **Social media:** YouTube (daily), Discord (daily), Reddit (browsing), Instagram (passive scrolling)

### News Consumption Behavior

**Current habits:**
- Hears about news from YouTube commentary channels
- Reads Reddit threads when something big happens
- Occasionally clicks news articles from Reddit but skims
- Sees headlines from Google News notifications (swipes away most)
- Gets info from Discord friend groups
- Actively avoids traditional news ("it's all biased anyway")

**Information diet:**
- 📱 Screen time: 5-6 hours/day
- 🎮 Gaming: 2-3 hours/day
- 📰 Time on news: < 10 min/day (mostly accidental)
- 🎥 Content preference: Long-form video essays, gaming content

**Pain points:**
- "News is depressing, why would I seek that out?"
- "Everything is political now, I just want to chill"
- "I don't even know what's real anymore so why bother"
- "My parents watch cable news and it's all yelling"
- "School makes us do current events but it's so boring"

### Motivations & Goals

**Why he'd use NewMoji:**
- ✅ Low-friction way to know "enough" to not seem ignorant
- ✅ Wants to form opinions on things he cares about (tech, environment, gaming)
- ✅ Curious about the world, just doesn't want to be overwhelmed
- ✅ Likes learning when it's interactive (plays educational games)
- ✅ Feels like he should be more informed but doesn't know how

**What would make him return:**
- 🎮 Gamification that feels like actual games (progression, achievements)
- ⚡ Super low time commitment (5 min max)
- 🎯 Can choose topics he cares about
- 🧠 Feels like he's actually learning something useful
- 🚫 Not preachy or trying to change his politics
- 📊 Data/stats shown visually (he likes charts, infographics)

### Digital Behavior Patterns

**App usage:**
- Only has apps he actively uses (no clutter)
- Checks phone between gaming sessions
- Watches YouTube during meals
- Passive scroller (rarely posts or comments)
- Deletes apps quickly if they're annoying

**Content engagement:**
- Prefers depth over breadth (will watch 45-min video essay)
- Skips anything that feels like marketing or manipulation
- Respects creators who admit what they don't know
- Allergic to clickbait
- Likes content that explains "why" not just "what"

**Learning style:**
- Hands-on, interactive
- Responds to logic and evidence
- Skeptical by default
- Likes systems and frameworks
- Hates being talked down to

### Quotes
- "I'll learn about news stuff if it's relevant to me, otherwise why"
- "Every news source has an agenda so I just ignore all of it"
- "YouTube actually explains things better than news articles"
- "My teacher made us watch CNN in class and it was so cringe"
- "I know I should care more but everything feels hopeless anyway"

### NewMoji Experience (How he'd use it)

**Discovery:**
- Sees Reddit post: "This app actually made me care about news"
- Skeptical but clicks out of curiosity
- Tries it with low expectations

**First session:**
- Scrolls past emoji headlines (thinks it's gimmicky)
- Sees one about AI/tech: `🤖🧠💻 AI breakthrough`
- Taps it, reads summary
- "Oh this is actually explaining things clearly"
- Tries the interactive challenge (spotting bias)
- Gets it right, feels smart
- "Okay maybe this isn't as dumb as I thought"

**Return behavior:**
- Doesn't think about it for a few days
- Gets notification about tech story
- Opens it, reads 2-3 stories
- Realizes he's actually understanding things
- Media literacy score goes up
- "Huh, I'm actually getting better at this"

**Long-term:**
- Uses it sporadically (not daily)
- But when he does, engages deeply
- Completes learning modules
- Starts fact-checking things his parents say
- Actually volunteers to do current events presentation at school

### Design Implications
- Can't feel gimmicky or childish
- Needs substance behind the fun
- Interactive challenges must be actually challenging
- Show data/stats/visualizations
- Respect his intelligence (no ELI5 tone)
- Topics he cares about must be prominent
- Let him skip the hand-holding after onboarding
- Achievement system should feel earned

## Persona 3: "Earnest Learner Alex"

### Demographics
- **Age:** 15
- **Occupation:** High school freshman (honors student)
- **Location:** Mid-sized city, lives with parents
- **Tech:** iPhone (hand-me-down from older sibling), iPad for homework
- **Social media:** TikTok (watches, rarely posts), YouTube (for homework help), Snapchat (friends only)

### News Consumption Behavior

**Current habits:**
- Reads news articles assigned for school
- Watches local news with parents sometimes
- Googles topics when confused about what everyone's talking about
- Follows a few news accounts on TikTok
- Asks parents/teachers questions about news
- Genuinely wants to understand but doesn't know what sources to trust

**Information diet:**
- 📱 Screen time: 3-4 hours/day
- 📚 Homework: 2-3 hours/day
- 📰 Time on news: 15-20 min/day (mostly for school)
- 🎥 Content preference: Educational videos, then text

**Pain points:**
- "News articles use words I don't understand"
- "I don't have enough background to understand what's happening"
- "My parents explain things differently than my teachers"
- "I want to have my own opinions but don't know enough yet"
- "How do I know if a source is trustworthy?"

### Motivations & Goals

**Why they'd use NewMoji:**
- ✅ Wants to actually understand current events for school and life
- ✅ Interested in forming independent opinions (not just adopting parents' views)
- ✅ Enjoys learning when it's well-explained
- ✅ Wants to participate in conversations about news
- ✅ Genuinely curious about the world

**What would make them return:**
- 📚 Clear explanations without assuming prior knowledge
- 🎯 Helps with school assignments/discussions
- 🧠 Feels like they're actually getting smarter
- ✅ Can verify what they're learning is accurate
- 🏆 Achievement system that tracks learning progress
- 👨‍🏫 Educational but not boring

### Digital Behavior Patterns

**App usage:**
- Uses apps for specific purposes (not mindless scrolling)
- Checks Snapchat for friends, YouTube for learning
- Actually reads notifications before dismissing
- Keeps apps organized in folders
- Parents monitor screen time somewhat

**Content engagement:**
- Will read full articles if interested
- Takes notes on things they want to remember
- Asks questions in comments
- Subscribes to educational creators
- Prefers structured learning to random scrolling

**Learning style:**
- Sequential (needs foundation before advanced concepts)
- Verbal and visual combined
- Asks "why" a lot
- Likes examples and analogies
- Responds well to positive reinforcement

### Quotes
- "I want to understand what's happening but news is written for adults"
- "How do you know if something on the internet is true or not?"
- "My friend said something about politics and I didn't know what to say"
- "I wish there was a way to learn about news that wasn't boring"
- "My mom gets her news from Facebook and I don't think that's good"

### NewMoji Experience (How they'd use it)

**Discovery:**
- Teacher mentions app in class as optional resource
- Parents approve (looks educational)
- Downloads it to try for homework assignment

**First session:**
- Opens app, sees emoji headlines
- Thinks "is this for little kids?"
- Clicks one anyway: `🌍🛢️⚡ Energy crisis`
- Level 1 is simple, Level 2 adds context
- "Oh this is actually teaching me"
- Sees explanation of bias in headlines
- Has lightbulb moment

**Return behavior:**
- Uses it for current events homework every week
- Parents notice they're asking smarter questions
- Starts using it outside of homework (actually interested)
- Completes media literacy modules
- Shows friends the "spot the bias" games

**Long-term:**
- Daily user (part of evening routine)
- Completes all educational content
- Media literacy score in top 10%
- Uses skills to evaluate sources for school papers
- Teaches younger sibling how to spot fake news
- Parents are impressed, tell other parents

### Design Implications
- Clear learning progression
- Definitions/explanations of complex terms
- Context and background for every story
- Can't be too childish (they're 15, not 10)
- But also can't assume too much prior knowledge
- Educational content must be high quality
- Parents should be able to see learning progress
- Safe, no inappropriate content
- Could integrate with school curricula

## Design Implications Summary

### Universal needs across personas:
- ✅ Mobile-first design
- ✅ Progressive disclosure (respect attention span)
- ✅ Multiple entry points for different interests
- ✅ Visible learning progress
- ✅ Credibility indicators
- ✅ No condescension

### Persona-specific features:

**For Zoe (Social Media Native):**
- Shareable achievements to social media
- Daily streak notifications
- Fast, visual interface
- Social proof (what others are reading)

**For Cameron (Checked Out):**
- Topic filtering (tech, gaming, environment)
- Substantive challenges, not trivial
- Respectful tone
- Optional deep dives
- Can skip basics

**For Alex (Earnest Learner):**
- Structured learning paths
- Glossary/definitions
- Progress tracking parents can see
- Citation/source transparency
- Homework-friendly features

## User Testing Questions

When we test with real users, we need to validate:

**For each persona:**
1. Does the emoji headline hook them? (First 10 seconds)
2. Do they tap to see more? (Progressive disclosure works?)
3. Do they feel talked down to or respected?
4. Do they understand the media literacy concepts?
5. Would they return tomorrow? Why or why not?
6. What would make them share this with friends?
7. What feels gimmicky vs genuinely helpful?

**Red flags to watch for:**
- 🚩 "This is for kids" (from older teens)
- 🚩 "This is too complicated" (from younger users)
- 🚩 "Why would I use this instead of [TikTok/Reddit]?"
- 🚩 "This feels like homework"
- 🚩 Eye-rolling at educational content
- 🚩 Bouncing after first story

---

*These personas should guide every design decision. When choosing between options, ask: "What would Zoe/Cameron/Alex do?"*
