# User Testing Plan

## Overview
This document outlines how to test the NewMoji prototype with real Gen Z/Alpha users to validate core assumptions and identify issues before building the full product.

---

## Testing Goals

### Primary Questions to Answer
1. **Does the emoji headline hook them?** (First 10 seconds)
2. **Does progressive disclosure feel natural?** (Tap behavior)
3. **Is the educational content engaging or preachy?** (User reactions)
4. **Would they actually use this daily?** (Intent to return)
5. **Does it feel age-appropriate?** (Not childish, not boring)

### Secondary Questions
6. Can they spot bias after using it?
7. Do they understand the value proposition?
8. What would they change?
9. Would they recommend to friends?
10. What's their favorite/least favorite part?

---

## Test Plan

### Phase 1: Guerrilla Testing (Week 1)
**Goal:** Quick validation of core concepts
**Participants:** 10-15 users (ages 15-24)
**Duration:** 10-15 min per session
**Location:** Coffee shops, campus, library

### Phase 2: Moderated Testing (Week 2)
**Goal:** Deep insights and iteration
**Participants:** 8-10 users (mixed personas)
**Duration:** 30-45 min per session
**Location:** Scheduled video calls or in-person

### Phase 3: Unmoderated Testing (Week 3)
**Goal:** Natural behavior observation
**Participants:** 20-30 users
**Duration:** Use at home for 3 days
**Method:** Remote testing tool + survey

## Testing Protocol: Moderated Sessions

### Setup (Before participant arrives)
- Prototype loaded on mobile device (iPhone or Android)
- Recording software ready (with permission)
- Note-taking template prepared
- Quiet, comfortable environment

### Session Structure (30 min)

**Part 1: Introduction (3 min)**
```
"Hi! Thanks for helping us test this prototype for a news app.

There are no right or wrong answers - we're testing the app,
not you. Please think out loud as you use it, and be honest
about what you like and don't like.

Your feedback will help us make this better.

Before we start, I need your permission to record this session.
Your name won't be attached to the recording. Is that okay?

[Get consent, start recording]

Do you have any questions before we begin?"
```

**Part 2: Pre-Test Questions (2 min)**
```
Just a few quick questions:

1. How old are you?
2. Where do you typically get news? (TikTok, Instagram, etc.)
3. How often do you read or watch news?
4. What frustrates you about news?
5. Have you heard of media literacy before?
```

**Part 3: First Impressions (1 min)**
```
[Hand them device with prototype loaded on home screen]

"Take a look at this screen. Don't tap anything yet.
What do you think this app is for?"

[Wait for response, note their interpretation]

"What catches your eye first?"
"Does anything confuse you?"
```

**Part 4: Task 1 - Open a Story (10 min)**
```
"Okay, now you can use the app naturally. Pretend I'm not here.

Task: Choose a story that interests you and explore it.
Remember to think out loud - tell me what you're thinking."

[Observe silently, only prompt if they get stuck]

Things to watch for:
- Do they tap an emoji headline?
- How long until first tap?
- Do they smile or react to emoji?
- Do they read or skim?
- Do they tap to continue to next levels?
- Do they try the challenge?
- Do they seem engaged or bored?
- Where do they get confused?
```

**Part 5: Task 2 - Complete Challenge (5 min)**
```
[If they didn't try challenge naturally]

"Go back and try answering the challenge about bias."

[Watch how they approach it]

Questions after:
- Was that easy or hard?
- Did you learn something or already know it?
- Does this feel like homework or like a game?
```

**Part 6: Debrief (7 min)**
```
Great! Now I have some questions:

1. On a scale of 1-10, how likely would you use this daily?
   Why that score?

2. What did you like most about it?

3. What annoyed you or felt off?

4. The emoji headlines - childish or clever?

5. The educational parts - helpful or preachy?

6. Would you recommend this to a friend? Why or why not?

7. If you could change one thing, what would it be?

8. How much would you pay for this (if anything)?

9. Compared to getting news on TikTok, how does this feel?

10. Did this change how you think about reading news?
```

**Part 7: Wrap-Up (2 min)**
```
"Thank you so much! Your feedback is super helpful.

One last question: Can we follow up with you in a few weeks
to see if you'd try an updated version?"

[Collect contact info if they agree]

[Provide incentive]
```

## Testing Protocol: Guerrilla Sessions


## Observation Checklist

### During Testing, Note:

**Engagement Signals (Positive)**
- ✅ Smiles or laughs
- ✅ Leans in closer
- ✅ Reads carefully
- ✅ Tries challenge
- ✅ Explores multiple levels
- ✅ Comments positively
- ✅ Asks when they can use it

**Red Flags (Negative)**
- 🚩 Confused facial expressions
- 🚩 Skips content quickly
- 🚩 Taps aimlessly
- 🚩 Stops engaging after first story
- 🚩 Says "this is for kids"
- 🚩 Bounces back to home screen
- 🚩 Looks bored or distracted

### Specific Behaviors to Track

**First 30 seconds:**
- Time until first tap: ___ seconds
- First thing they tap: ___
- Emotional reaction: ___

**Progressive Disclosure:**
- Do they tap to Level 2? Yes/No
- Do they tap to Level 3? Yes/No
- Do they tap to Level 4? Yes/No
- Do they seem to understand the mechanic? Yes/No

**Challenge Engagement:**
- Do they attempt it? Yes/No
- Do they get it right? Yes/No
- Time spent on challenge: ___ seconds
- Reaction to result: ___

**Overall:**
- Total time in prototype: ___ min
- Stories viewed: ___
- Likelihood to use again (1-10): ___
- Net Promoter Score (1-10): ___

## Success Metrics

### Must Achieve (or pivot)
- **70%+ understand what the app is for** within 10 seconds
- **60%+ tap to Level 2** (progressive disclosure works)
- **40%+ try a challenge** (engage with education)
- **50%+ say they'd use it** (product-market fit signal)

### Moderate Success
- **30%+ read to Level 4** (deep engagement)
- **60%+ correctly answer challenge** (learning is happening)
- **40%+ would recommend to friend** (viral potential)
- **Average session: 3+ minutes**

### Red Flags to Worry About
- **< 50% understand purpose** → Positioning is wrong
- **< 40% tap to Level 2** → Progressive disclosure failing
- **< 20% try challenge** → Education isn't engaging
- **< 30% would use again** → No retention potential
- **"This is for kids" from 50%+** → Age targeting wrong

## Analysis Framework

### After Each Session
- Write summary notes immediately
- Tag key quotes and observations
- Note patterns emerging
- Identify critical issues to fix

### After 5 Sessions
- Look for repeated feedback
- Categorize issues by severity
- Prioritize what to fix first
- Update prototype if critical issues found

### After All Sessions
- Create summary report with:
  - Key findings (3-5 main insights)
  - Success metrics achieved
  - Critical issues to address
  - Nice-to-have improvements
  - Validation of core hypotheses
  - Recommended next steps

## Iteration Plan

### After Each Testing Phase

**Phase 1 Results → Quick Fixes (Week 1)**
- Fix critical UX bugs
- Adjust copy if confusing
- Tweak visual hierarchy
- Update for Phase 2 testing

**Phase 2 Results → Major Changes (Week 2)**
- Redesign problem areas
- Add missing features users expect
- Remove features that aren't working
- Refine educational approach

**Phase 3 Results → Final Decisions (Week 3)**
- Validate fixes worked
- Make go/no-go decision
- Finalize feature scope for MVP
- Create technical requirements doc

---

## Red Flags That Mean Pivot

If testing reveals:

❌ **"This is just news with emoji"**
→ Value prop isn't clear, education isn't working

❌ **"I'd just use TikTok"**
→ Not differentiated enough, no compelling reason to switch

❌ **"This is for little kids"**
→ Design/tone is wrong for target audience

❌ **"Why would I come back tomorrow?"**
→ No retention hook, habit loop isn't working

❌ **"The educational stuff is annoying"**
→ Core product premise is wrong

**If 3+ of these appear in majority of tests → Major pivot needed**

## Next Steps After Testing

### If Validation Succeeds (60%+ positive metrics):
1. Compile findings into product spec
2. Design remaining screens
3. Plan technical architecture (now we know what to build)
4. Create MVP development roadmap
5. Line up beta testers for launch

### If Mixed Results (40-60% positive):
1. Identify what's working vs what's not
2. Redesign problem areas
3. Re-test specific features
4. Iterate until confident

### If Validation Fails (< 40% positive):
1. Deep dive into why
2. Decide: Pivot concept or kill project
3. If pivot: Return to design thinking phase
4. If kill: Document learnings, move on
