# Progressive Disclosure Content Structure

## Overview
Progressive disclosure is the CORE mechanic of NewMoji. It respects short attention spans while gradually building to depth and education. Each level serves a specific purpose in the learning journey.

---

## The Four Levels

### Level 0: Emoji Headline (The Hook)
**Purpose:** Grab attention, spark curiosity
**Time to consume:** < 2 seconds
**Design:** Visual pattern recognition

### Level 1: Text Headline (The Entry)
**Purpose:** Clarify what the story is about
**Time to consume:** 3-5 seconds
**Design:** Plain language, no jargon

### Level 2: The Summary (The Facts)
**Purpose:** Explain what happened without bias
**Time to consume:** 15-30 seconds
**Design:** Neutral, factual, contextual

### Level 3: The Perspectives (The Education)
**Purpose:** Show how different sources frame it, teach media literacy
**Time to consume:** 1-2 minutes
**Design:** Multi-perspective, interactive, educational

### Level 4: The Deep Dive (The Resources)
**Purpose:** Link to original sources, related learning
**Time to consume:** 5+ minutes (optional rabbit hole)
**Design:** Curated links, learning modules, quizzes

## Example 1: Political/Economic Story

### Level 0: Emoji Headline
```
🔥💰📊 Congress spending drama
```

**Emoji breakdown:**
- 🔥 = Controversial/heated debate
- 💰 = Money/spending involved
- 📊 = Budget/economic policy

**Design notes:**
- Max 3-4 emoji per headline
- Must be instantly recognizable
- Pattern should tell a micro-story
- Different emoji combos for similar topics

---

### Level 1: Text Headline
```
Congress passes $1.2 trillion spending bill
```

**Writing guidelines:**
- Active voice
- No loaded language
- Who, what (not why/how yet)
- < 10 words
- 5th grade reading level

**Bad examples:**
- ❌ "Congress approves massive spending package" (loaded: "massive")
- ❌ "Lawmakers vote on fiscal legislation" (jargon: "fiscal legislation")
- ❌ "$1.2T spending bill passes along party lines" (too much info)

---

### Level 2: The Summary
```
Congress passed a $1.2 trillion spending bill to keep
the government funded through September 2024. The bill
includes $60 billion for disaster relief and $20 billion
for infrastructure projects, but cuts $15 billion from
climate programs.

The vote was 314-108 in the House and 68-29 in the Senate,
with bipartisan support.

💡 Notice how we presented just facts without emotional
   language or interpretation. That comes next.

[Tap to see how different sources covered this ↓]
```

**Writing guidelines:**
- 2-3 short paragraphs max
- Who, what, when, where
- Key numbers and facts
- No adjectives that signal bias
- No speculation about motivations
- One tiny teachable moment (the 💡)

**Structure:**
1. What happened (core event)
2. Key details (numbers, specifics)
3. Micro-lesson (highlighted)

```
  (10 questions, ~3 min)

─────────────────────────────────────

• 📊💵🏛️ Federal budget basics
• 🌍⚡💰 Climate spending debate
• 🏥💊📈 Infrastructure priorities

─────────────────────────────────────

💬 REFLECT:

After reading multiple perspectives, what do YOU think

[Write your thoughts]

```

**Design guidelines:**
- Links to original sources (traffic = partnerships)
- Educational content is modular and optional
- Related stories create rabbit holes
- Reflection prompt encourages critical thinking
- No echo chamber - all perspectives available

## Example 3: Climate/Environmental Story

### Level 0: Emoji Headline
```

```

---

### Level 1: Text Headline
```
Record heat and cold hit same week
```

```
This week, Texas hit 95°F (35°C) while Minnesota saw

Scientists say these extreme swings are consistent with
climate change predictions.

The temperature difference was 110 degrees within 1,000
miles - the largest spread ever recorded in the U.S.
in a single day.

💡 Notice we said "consistent with climate change" not
   "caused by climate change." Scientists are careful
```

```
💡 UNDERSTANDING THE DIFFERENCE:

Weather = What's happening right now
Climate = Patterns over decades

A single extreme weather event ≠ climate change
BUT: More frequent extreme events = climate pattern

This is why scientists say "consistent with" rather
than "caused by." They're being scientifically precise.

🎓 Learn the difference: Weather vs Climate (3 min)

─────────────────────────────────────

🧪 THE SCIENCE:

What scientists actually say:
"Climate change is increasing the probability and
intensity of extreme weather events. We can't say
this specific event was caused by climate change,
but we can say events like this are becoming more
common and more extreme as the planet warms."

Sources: NOAA, NASA, IPCC reports

📊 See the data: Temperature extremes over time

⚖️ WHY THIS IS CONTROVERSIAL:

• Some believe climate change is urgent crisis
• Others think it's exaggerated or natural cycles
• Economic interests (fossil fuels vs renewables)
• Political identity has become tied to position
• Scientific consensus vs public perception gap

This makes it hard to have productive conversations.
Understanding ALL perspectives helps.
```

## Content Guidelines for All Levels

### Voice & Tone
- **Conversational but not condescending**
  - ✅ "Notice how..."
  - ❌ "You might not know this, but..."

- **Educational but not preachy**
  - ✅ "This is how bias works..."
  - ❌ "You should always check multiple sources!"

- **Curious, not judgmental**
  - ✅ "Different people see this differently because..."
  - ❌ "The right answer is..."

### Reading Level
- **Level 1-2:** 5th-7th grade reading level
- **Level 3:** 7th-9th grade reading level
- **Level 4:** 9th-11th grade (but with definitions)

### Word Count Targets
- **Level 0:** 3-4 emoji
- **Level 1:** 5-10 words
- **Level 2:** 75-150 words
- **Level 3:** 200-400 words
- **Level 4:** Varies (modular content)

### Time to Consume
- **Level 0-1:** 5 seconds combined
- **Level 2:** 30-45 seconds
- **Level 3:** 2-3 minutes
- **Level 4:** 5-15 minutes (optional deep dive)

### Interactive Elements
- **Level 2:** One micro-lesson (💡)
- **Level 3:** 1-2 interactive challenges
- **Level 4:** Multiple learning modules, quizzes, reflection

## Story Selection Criteria

### What makes a good NewMoji story?

✅ **Clear multiple perspectives exist**
- Not everything is controversial
- Need genuine different viewpoints

✅ **Relevant to target audience**
- Why would 15-24 year olds care?
- Affects their lives or future

✅ **Teachable media literacy moment**
- Can we show how bias works?
- Can we teach source evaluation?

✅ **Not too complex for entry level**
- Can be explained in 150 words?
- Not requiring extensive background

✅ **Timely but not outdated quickly**
- Current events preferred
- Evergreen stories also valuable

## Content Production Workflow

1. **Source Identification**
   - Monitor NewsData.io API
   - Editorial curation for quality
   - Ensure story has multiple perspectives

2. **Level 0-1 Creation** (AI-assisted)
   - Generate emoji patterns
   - Write neutral headline
   - Human review required

3. **Level 2 Summary** (AI + human)
   - Claude generates summary
   - Editor verifies neutrality and accuracy
   - Add micro-lesson callout

4. **Level 3 Perspectives** (Human-curated)
   - Find 3-5 different source headlines
   - Categorize by perspective
   - Write educational callouts
   - Design interactive challenge

5. **Level 4 Resources** (Semi-automated)
   - Link to original sources
   - Tag related learning modules
   - Add quiz questions
   - Include reflection prompts
